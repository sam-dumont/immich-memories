# Your first film

Reader: newcomer.

One month, start to finish, in the web UI, then the same thing on the CLI. A month is the right
first cut: about a minute of film, and the slow part (reading each picture once) is bounded by
what one month holds.

This page assumes the [Quick start](./quick-start.md) is done: the container is up and
`models fetch` has run. On pip, it's the same commands without the
`docker compose exec immich-memories` prefix: [uv or pip](../run/uv-pip.md).

## Pick a month

Open [http://localhost:8080](http://localhost:8080). With `IMMICH_URL` and `IMMICH_API_KEY` set,
the page reads Immich on its own and shows the brief. If it doesn't, open **Settings**, fill in
**Immich Connection** and press **Test Connection**.

Choose **Monthly Highlights**, a year and a month with a fair number of pictures and a few videos.
Leave the length empty: it is fitted to what the month can fill.

<ThemedScreenshot name="memory-brief" alt="The brief, connected to Immich" />

## Cut

Press **Cut**. The panel follows the run: reading dates, places and people, then the pictures,
then the edit. A stage that counts its work draws a bar with an estimate, and the last pictures it
read go past underneath. The cut runs on the server, so reloading the page mid-cut picks it back up.

If `immich-memories models fetch` has not run on this host, the cut does not start and nothing is
asked of Immich. The panel shows the command's own last lines, which name the missing model and
the command to run (`Run immich-memories models fetch`), and they stay there after a reload.

<ThemedScreenshot name="memory-cutting" alt="The cut in progress" />

The first cut reads every picture the film can reach: the month's pictures, their Live Photo clips
and the bursts around them. Each one goes through the eight context heads and the two detectors
once, and the answers are banked. The second cut of the same month reads nothing again.

## Read the cut

When the cut finishes, its review opens as a contact sheet in playback order. Open a picture to see
it larger and the reason it stayed; a video plays the stretch the film uses. **Stories** shows the
same cut the way the editor weighed it, heaviest story first.

<ThemedScreenshot name="memory-story" alt="The contact sheet of the first cut" />

A few things to look for on a first cut:

- **It's chronological.** Always. A film never jumps back in time.
- **Favourites come first.** A picture you starred in Immich wins its moment over the others.
- **Videos lead.** When a video carries a moment, it beats a still of the same moment.
- **Quiet weeks can be empty.** A week at home with no favourite, no video, no close family and no
  unusually busy day gets no shot. The film goes short rather than fill it with a guess.

Why each rule exists, and every number behind it, is on
[How it chooses](../how-it-chooses/overview.md).

## Fix it

Two ways, depending on what's wrong.

A shot is wrong but the film is right: edit it in place. In the inspector, **Remove from this cut**,
trim a video with **Start here** and **End here**, or **Use this picture instead** on another
picture of the same moment. **Save revision** keeps the edits; nothing is re-selected.

<ThemedScreenshot name="memory-review-edit" alt="A picture removed and one swapped, with the change bar" />

The editor should think again: open **Pool**. It shows every picture the cut saw, ticked if the cut
kept it. Untick a picture the editor kept and it's out; tick one it dropped and it's in, in its own
story at its own time. Press **Cut again with these choices**. The one thing that outranks a tick is
the family-viewing gate. Twins and bursts the editor judges itself; what it can't know is what you
would never show anyone, and **Never use** is for that.

<ThemedScreenshot name="memory-pool-outcomes" alt="The pool after a cut, with the picks ticked" />

More ways to steer it (stars, people, `runs why`) are on
[Overrule it](../how-it-chooses/overrule-it.md).

## Render and watch

Scroll to **Render**. Pick the cut or a saved revision under **What to render**, and press **Render**.
Resolution, title and music are all there, each defaulting to your configuration; the defaults are
fine for a first film. The panel walks through downloading the clips, assembling with titles and
transitions, the music, and delivery.

<ThemedScreenshot name="memory-export" alt="The rendered film playing on the review page" />

The film plays on the page when it's done. It's in `./output` beside your compose file on Docker,
`~/Videos/Memories` on pip, one folder per run. With **Upload the film to Immich** ticked, the film
also goes to the Immich album you named, tagged as this app's own so a later run never films it
again.

## The same on the CLI

```bash
alias im='docker compose exec immich-memories immich-memories'
im generate --memory-type monthly_highlights --year 2025 --month 6
im runs story        # the storyboard of the last run, in the terminal
im runs why <asset-id>   # what the run decided about one picture, and why
```

`generate` prepares, cuts and renders in one go. To read a bigger window ahead of time (a whole
year, overnight on a NAS), run `im prepare --year 2025`: it reads and banks, and cuts nothing.
Every cut inside that year then starts warm. All flags: [generate](../make/cli/generate.md),
[prepare](../make/cli/prepare.md).
